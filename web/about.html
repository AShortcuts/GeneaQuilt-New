About the project

See the shape of your family story.

GeneaQuilt turns a GEDCOM family tree into a compact, interactive quilt. It is made for the moment when a normal pedigree chart becomes too wide, too tangled, or too hard to hold in your head.

Purpose and strengths

A map for the whole family network

A standard pedigree is excellent for following one ancestral line. GeneaQuilt is strongest when you want to understand an entire GEDCOM—many lines, marriages, siblings, disconnected groups, and generations—without repeatedly drawing the same person.

i

Think of GeneaQuilt as a map, not the archive. Keep your master research in a family-tree program; use this site to explore its structure.

01

One coherent view at serious scale

The original Java system loaded and visualized up to 10,000 person-and-family nodes at interactive rates, including genealogies with thousands of people and roughly 80 generations.

02

Every person can remain in the map

The layout is not rooted on one chosen person. It can place every loaded person and family, including disconnected components that are unrelated to the main group.

03

Shared ancestors are not drawn again and again

Each person has one row and each nuclear family one column. Pedigree collapse, intermarriage, and relinking paths converge on the same record instead of recursively cloning boxes.

04

Fast overview; detail when requested

Fit, overview, search, selection, relationship tracing, and focus controls reveal context without forcing every label and control onto the screen.

An honest qualification: the 10,000-node result belongs to the original 2010 Java research implementation, not a published benchmark for this browser port. Screen space also becomes the limiting factor when a single generation contains hundreds of names, even if the layout itself remains responsive.

Visualization landscape

Can anything else show the whole tree?

Several methods handle scale or repeated ancestry well, but they answer different questions. The important test is whether a view keeps the full network, makes pedigree collapse understandable, and still lets you recognize individual people.

“Without recursion” means without redrawing the same ancestor for every path. Genealogists usually call the underlying pattern pedigree collapse or a relinking marriage. A genealogy is therefore a graph, not always a clean tree. GeneaQuilt gives each person one row, so converging paths return to that one person instead of producing duplicate boxes.

Closest overall fit

GeneaQuilts

What it captures The whole loaded dataset, disconnected components, and shared ancestry in one organized matrix; each person and nuclear family appears once.

What it gives up Names become dense at extreme scale. Zoom, search, filtering, and detail-on-demand are still needed.

Structural analysis

P-graphs, Pajek, and Puck

What they capture Large kinship networks, marriage rings, and relinking patterns exceptionally well.

What they give up They are expert analytical tools; names, chronology, and the story of a particular person can get lost.

Very large overview

Clustered and fractal layouts

What they capture Recent TU Wien research handled multiple roots and cycles and tested a Habsburg dataset of more than 30,000 people.

What they give up Clustering or subdividing descendant regions gains scale by reducing how much person-level detail remains readable at once.

Focused browsing

Dual Trees and H-tree pedigrees

What they capture Compact ancestor or descendant exploration; H-tree views can mark duplicate ancestors and loops.

What they give up They show a focus, a subset, or one person’s ancestry—not every connected and disconnected person at once.

Familiar small trees

Pedigree, fan, and hourglass charts

What they capture A clear, familiar story around one person or a modest number of generations.

What they give up Shared ancestors are commonly duplicated, while remarriages, crossings, and width grow quickly.

My conclusion: GeneaQuilts remains unusual in balancing a whole-dataset overview, one representation per person, disconnected groups, and readable pedigree-collapse patterns. I did not find another established method that does all of those while keeping tens of thousands of named people legible in one static viewport. New clustered layouts push the scale farther, but trade away some person-level clarity. This is an inference from the surveyed research, not proof that no other method exists. GEDCOM basics

The travel format for family trees

GEDCOM stands for GEnealogical Data COMmunication. It is a structured plain-text format used to move family-history data between different programs.

Usually pronounced “JED-com.” The standard is maintained by FamilySearch.

What it usually carries

  • ✓ People, names, dates, and places
  • ✓ Parent, spouse, and child relationships
  • ✓ Events, notes, and many source references
  • ✓ Stable IDs that connect records together

A GEDCOM is an exchange copy, not always a perfect backup. Custom fields, formatting, media, and app-specific features can be changed or omitted during a transfer. Keep your program’s native backup too.

1

Export a .ged file from your usual family-tree app.

2

Open that copy in GeneaQuilt. The original file is not changed.

3

Inspect the quilt, then return to your main app to correct the research.

Learn more from the official GEDCOM project. GeneaQuilt currently reads GEDCOM text; it does not display photos or open a GEDZIP media bundle.

What is Bring-and-Slide?

It is a navigation idea carried over from the original GeneaQuilt. Select a person, then drag left to bring parents and siblings into view or right to bring spouses and children into view. The quilt moves while the selected context stays understandable.

A dependable workflow

Keep one master tree, several safe copies, and a clear purpose

GeneaQuilt works best as one step in a careful research practice—not as the only place your family history exists.

  1. 01

    Document in your main app

    Add sources, citations, notes, media, and uncertainty where the software can preserve them fully.

  2. 02

    Back up in two formats

    Keep the app’s native backup plus a dated GEDCOM export. Store media separately if it is not embedded.

  3. 03

    Explore a copy here

    Use the quilt to spot structural patterns, isolated groups, and branches that need a closer look.

  4. 04

    Correct the evidence, not just the picture

    A visualization shows what the file claims; it cannot prove that a relationship or date is accurate.

3·2·1

A useful backup rule

Keep three copies, on two different kinds of storage, with one copy in another safe location.

Privacy and sharing

Family files deserve the same care as family papers

A tree may contain living people’s names, dates, addresses, notes, and relationships. Decide who should see an export before you send or publish it.

01

While you explore

The selected GEDCOM is read in your browser and is not sent to GeneaQuilt for processing.

02

Before you share

Use your source app’s privacy controls to remove or obscure living people and sensitive notes.

03

After you export

Interactive HTML, PDFs, and screenshots can contain family data. Treat them as copies of the tree.

Does GeneaQuilt change my GEDCOM file?

No. It reads the copy you choose. Changes made while exploring are not written back to your original family-tree database.

Can I build or edit my family tree here?

Not currently. GeneaQuilt is intentionally an explorer. Use one of the programs above to maintain people, events, sources, media, and corrections.

Why did a detail disappear after a GEDCOM transfer?

Programs implement different GEDCOM versions and extensions. Some proprietary fields have no exact equivalent, so compare totals and important records after every migration.

Is a bigger screen required?

The site and this guide work on phones and tablets, but a large, dense quilt is naturally easier to study on a laptop or desktop display.

Origins, credit, and scope

A browser port of an original research idea

This project builds on the original Java GeneaQuilts project by Jean-Daniel Fekete, Pierre Dragicevic, and INRIA. Their work introduced the diagonally filled matrix, overview, timeline, search, and Bring-and-Slide ideas preserved here.
